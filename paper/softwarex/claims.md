## Discrepancies

42 ledger rows differ. They come down to 25 issues. Each issue below gives its IDs, the claimed and
computed values and my reading of the cause. Nothing here is fixed: the decisions belong to the
interrogation (4.2). The reference list has its own report, `references_check.md`: two entries
are never cited, and the APOLLO title is printed with a prefix that the registered title lacks.

### A. Numbers that differ

1. **C009** (abstract): "up to 238,492 genomes × 5,844 reactions". No table has those dimensions.
   The largest table is 238,492 × 5,129 (combined); 5,844 reactions occur only in the
   sequenced-genome tables (at most 147,022 rows). *Cause:* two maxima from different tables were
   joined into one.
2. **C014, C151** (abstract, §3.2): "1.4% of them or fewer" on the public sets. Computed: **1.8%**.
   Gisette keeps 89.3 of 5,000 = 1.79%. 1.4% is Madelon's figure (7.1 of 500). *Cause:* the maximum
   was taken from Madelon. CHECKPOINT.md has the same figure, so it was carried over from there.
3. **C030** (§1): "a random forest on all columns reaches 99% accuracy on the tables used here".
   Computed: 97.1–99.8% on the ten reconstruction tables (combined order 97.07, genus 97.58,
   sequenced-genome order and genus 97.87); 80.4–96.1% on the public sets. *Cause:* true for the
   phylum and body-site tables only.
4. **C041** (§1): "with ten or five random splits each". Computed: 1, 5 or 10. *Cause:* the six
   half tables ran one seed, which the sentence leaves out.
5. **C052** (§2.1): "89 tests". Computed: **92** passed, 99% coverage. *Cause:* the benchmark tests
   were added after the count. The red note already says to recount at release.
6. **C019** (Table 1): version 1.0.0. The code says 1.0.0.dev0; the release is not tagged yet (red
   note).
7. **C182, C185, C194** (Table 3, §3.2): Boruta on combined phylum 936 s, computed 935 (935.49);
   RFECV on Madelon 30 s, computed 29 (29.47); hence "30 s to 6 min for RFECV" is 29 s.
   *Cause:* double rounding, first to one decimal (935.5, 29.5) and then to whole seconds.
8. **C233, C234, C235, C237** (Table 4, Peak GB): printed 3.7 / 3.8 / 3.7 / 2.9, computed
   3.8 / 3.9 / 3.8 / 3.0 (the two order rows of the halves agree). *Cause:* `peak_rss_mb` is
   decimal MB (`ru_maxrss` bytes / 1e6 on macOS), and the table divided it by 1,024. That gives
   neither GB (3.8, 3.9, 3.8, 3.8, 3.0, 2.7, as in CHECKPOINT.md) nor GiB (3.6, 3.7, 3.6, 3.6, 2.8,
   2.5). "Below 4 GB" (C227) holds in either unit.
9. **C253** (§5): "met its validation bound in every one of the 484 benchmark runs". Computed: the
   bound applies only to FastForest's own runs, 122 of the 484. It held in **61/61 MDI runs**
   (the default) and **40/61 permutation runs**: 101/122. *Cause:* 484 counts every method, and
   `bound_satisfied` is empty for the other methods and was not split by variant. The checkpoint
   record of 2026-10-17 in the notebook makes the same "all 484 runs" claim. The body-site claim
   (C108, 10/10) is right.
10. **C192** (§3.2): "FastForest's selection took 1.5–98 s on the 20,000-row tables". On the
    20,000-row tables it took 23–98 s. 1.5 s is Madelon (2,600 rows). *Cause:* the range spans all
    seven tables of Table 3, but the qualifier names only the 20,000-row ones. The Boruta, RFECV and
    L1 ranges in the same sentence also span all seven tables (on the 20,000-row tables alone,
    Boruta takes 16–26 min).
11. **C191** (Fig. 2 caption): "one baseline is slightly more accurate" on the public benchmarks.
    Computed: 1 on Madelon (Boruta), **3 on Gisette** (Boruta 96.49, RFECV 96.59, L1 96.50 against
    96.20) and **2 on GEMLeR** (Boruta 96.41, L1 96.50 against 96.08). *Cause:* the caption
    generalises from the best-baseline comparison in §3.2.

### B. Statements the results or the code do not support as worded

12. **C012, C143** (abstract, §3.2): "at equal or better accuracy" on the reaction-presence tables.
    Holds for 7 of 9 table–baseline pairs. On combined genus FastForest's 97.52 is below Boruta's
    97.60 and RFECV's 97.56 (and All's 97.58). The gaps are within one SD (0.17–0.26), so "equal"
    holds statistically but not at the printed precision.
13. **C017, C248** (abstract, §4): "less stable than Boruta". True on 6 of 7 tables. On GEMLeR
    FastForest is the more stable (0.43 against 0.35). The stability paragraph says so (C202);
    the abstract and §4 generalise.
14. **C018, C204, C249** (abstract, §3.2, §4): "the package reports [it] as a stable core" and
    "the package reports, alongside each run, the size of the selected set across seeds". Neither
    `fastforest/` nor `app/` has any multi-seed or core code. The core and the set sizes across
    seeds come from the benchmark's `summarize.py` (stability.csv). *Cause:* benchmark output
    described as a package feature. This connects to the open question of a stability-selection
    option (the red note at L230).
15. **C044, C097** (§2.1, listing comment): the result holds "the environment" and its JSON
    "parameters, metrics, timings, environment". `FastForestResult` has no environment field, and
    `to_json` writes the FastForest version only. The dependency versions are added by the CLI
    (`metrics.json` → `environment`), so C086 holds for the CLI.
16. **C066, C073** (Fig. 1 caption, §2.2): "the test split is evaluated once, by the final model"
    / "touched once, by the final model". `FastForest.fit` evaluates the full, S4 and final models
    on the test split in its last pass, and the CLI writes all three test confusion matrices. No
    decision uses those numbers, so the separation claims hold (C004, C065, C072). Only the words
    "by the final model" are wrong.
17. **C098** (§2.3): "The same run from a shell". The listing runs on the bundled 1,000 × 500
    subsample (11 features); the shell command runs on the full 1,957 × 8,306 table (20 features,
    test accuracy 0.9974).
18. **C112, C258** (§3.2, Data availability): "all per-run results are in the repository
    (`results/summary/`)". `results/` is git-ignored, and no file under it is tracked.
19. **C113, C259, C246** (§3.2, Data availability, §4): `reproduce.sh` "regenerates every table and
    figure" and "reproduces every number in this paper with one command". It reruns the benchmark
    and rebuilds `results/summary/` (its CSVs and the benchmark's own figures). It does not build
    the paper's figures (`paper/softwarex/make_figures.py`), and it needs the APOLLO Parquet files,
    which are not in git. The numbers in the text are typed from the CSVs. Not rerun here (about two
    days of computing).
20. **C189, C261** (Fig. 2 and Fig. S1 captions): "FastForest sits at the left edge of every
    panel". FastForest (MDI) keeps the fewest features in 1 of 7 panels (GEMLeR) and in 0 of 6
    half-table panels. All eight methods are plotted, and the permutation variant is further left
    everywhere except Madelon, where L1 is (1.1 features). *Cause:* true against Boruta, RFECV, L1
    and All, not against every plotted method.
21. **C161** (§3.2): "FastForest with permutation importance kept fewer features still". Not on
    GEMLeR (14.3 against 12.5): 6 of 7 tables.
22. **C197, C198** (§3.2): "Most of FastForest's time is the initial forest; the threshold search
    … is cheap by comparison". This holds on the full tables (initial forest 63–92% of the time) and
    on the phylum, order, body-site and public tables. It fails at genus level: on the combined genus
    table the search takes 64 s against 18 s for the initial forest (3.5×), and the forest is 30% of
    the 98 s; gut-MAG genus 1.7×. The timings are in `results/runs/*/fastforest_mdi/*.json`.
23. **C032** (§1): Boruta "finds every feature that is more informative than its own shuffled
    copy". Boruta tests each feature against the best of all shadow features (`perc = 100` in the
    configuration, as in Kursa & Rudnicki 2010), not against its own shuffled copy.
24. **C055** (§2.1): "a regression test for every defect". The tests name 6 of the 12 fixed bugs
    (B1, B2, B4, B5, B8, B9). B3, B6, B7 and B12 are covered by tests that do not name them; B10
    (`st.cache`) only through the app smoke test; no test was found for B11 (the global warnings
    filter).
25. **C091** (§2.2): the hosted app at fastforest.vmh.life answered **502** on 2026-10-27
    (Cloudflare reaches no origin). The red note already says to confirm it at submission.

## How to read and rerun

- **section** names the part of the paper and the line of `main.tex` (v0.1) the statement is on.
- **statement** is the clause verbatim from the LaTeX source; `claims_check.py` checks that it is
  still on that line and reports where it moved to.
- **claimed value** is the value the paper states, in the form the check prints. `True` means the
  claim holds as operationalised in the note.
- **how to check** is the snippet that is run. Python snippets are evaluated in the namespace of
  `claims_check.py`: `S` = `summary.csv` indexed by (dataset, method), `P` = `per_run.csv`,
  `ST` = `stability.csv` by (dataset, method), `R` = `recovery.csv`, `D` = `datasets.csv` by dataset,
  `SC` = `scalability.csv` by dataset, `ENV` = `results/env.json`, `CFG` = `benchmarks/config.yaml`,
  `PYPROJECT` = `pyproject.toml`; table groups `COMBINED` (3 combined APOLLO tables), `HALVES` (6),
  `RP` (9 reaction-presence), `RECON` (10 reconstruction), `PUBLIC` (3), `MAIN` (the 7 of Table 3),
  `ALL13`; `FF` = `fastforest_mdi`; helpers (`acc`, `nf`, `sec`, `rng`, `ratios`, `listing()`, `cli()`,
  `tests()`, …) are documented in the script. Snippets starting with `$` are shell commands run from
  the repository root.
- **computed value** is what the snippet printed on the last run. A trailing parenthesis is
  evidence and is ignored in the comparison.
- **status**: `reproduced` (equal to the printed precision), `differs`, `not checkable` (the note
  says why), `by construction`. Slow checks (`tests()`, `listing()`, `cli()`) run the test suite,
  the paper's sample code verbatim and its shell command; `--skip-slow` keeps their last values.
